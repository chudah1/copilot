# penKeep positioning

## Category

penKeep is a private investment decision record for self-directed investors.

Monitoring is the mechanism, not the category. The product connects four things
that other tools leave scattered: what the investor believed, what evidence
changed, what they decided, and what happened afterward.

## Canonical line

> Your broker records the trade. penKeep remembers the decision.

## Product promise

Record what you believed before hindsight edits the story. When evidence tests
that belief, penKeep returns the original decision with the relevant facts. The
call stays yours, and the outcome becomes part of a private record that gets more
useful over time.

## Initial customer

Self-directed, fundamentally minded investors who:

- own several individual companies rather than only passive funds;
- already read earnings, filings, newsletters, or company research;
- want to make their own decisions rather than receive stock tips;
- care about improving their process across years, not generating daily activity.

UK and European investors are the initial wedge, supported by GBP-native product
language, Trading 212, and coverage beyond US-only listings.

## Message hierarchy

1. **Decision memory:** Preserve the context that existed when the investor acted.
2. **Evidence in context:** Filter new information through recorded assumptions.
3. **Decision accountability:** Keep the next call beside the evidence that caused it.
4. **Long-term learning:** Return to the call after reality has tested it and reveal patterns over time.

## Differentiation

- Brokers record transactions; penKeep records the reasoning behind them.
- Alert products explain what happened today; penKeep connects it to an earlier belief and later outcome.
- AI chats answer a question in the moment; penKeep maintains the record across decision points.
- Journals store notes; penKeep actively returns the right note when evidence makes it relevant.
- Stock-picking products outsource judgment; penKeep preserves and improves the investor's own judgment.

## Voice

- Plain, direct, and written like a person explaining the product.
- Serious without sounding institutional, academic, or polished for its own sake.
- Use concrete words: belief, condition, evidence, decision, outcome, record.
- Treat silence as a product benefit.
- Never imply certainty, prediction, or that penKeep makes the decision.
- Prefer "what you wrote, what changed, and what you did next" over abstract
  phrases about accountability, intelligence, insight, or decision quality.
- Read every line aloud. If it sounds like generic AI-written SaaS copy, cut it.

## Avoid leading with

- "AI-powered portfolio monitoring"
- "Know when your thesis breaks"
- "Never forget why you bought"
- generic claims about smarter investing
- "unlock", "reveal", "transform", "seamless", "powerful", or "AI-powered"
- empty brand language such as "compounding advantage" or "outsourced judgment"
- feature lists before the decision-record promise
- engagement, streaks, hot stocks, or fear-based urgency

Those phrases either collapse penKeep into a crowded alert category or describe
only one step of the larger decision loop.

## Offer

- Free: up to five active theses and the core decision-record workflow.
- Founding Membership: £99 per year, with the rate locked while membership remains active.
- No lifetime plan: monitoring, data, and model costs continue for as long as the service runs.

Pricing remains an experiment until real customers demonstrate willingness to
pay. Copy should not present a temporary price test as settled strategy.

## Validation question

The commercial assumption is not merely that investors like the idea. It is:

> Will self-directed investors pay recurring money for a record that helps them
> inspect and improve their decisions over time?

The next proof is paid founding memberships and repeated use through real
earnings or review events—not waitlist signups or compliments.
